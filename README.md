# Smart Waste

Final project for the Building AI course

## Summary
Urban areas are increasingly plagued by waste management challenges such as overflowing bins, irregular collection, and poor waste segregation. These issues contribute to serious health hazards, environmental degradation, and strain on sanitation infrastructure. Personally witnessing the growing heaps of uncollected and unsorted garbage in my city has sparked my interest in exploring innovative solutions, particularly the potential of AI to bring efficiency and intelligence into the waste management system. Addressing this problem is crucial—not only to protect public health and the environment but also to improve the overall quality of urban life.

## Background

## How is it used?
1. Waste Segregation at Source
Encourage residents to separate waste into biodegradable, recyclable, and non-recyclable.
Provide color-coded bins and awareness programs.
Enforce segregation through local regulations.

✅ 2. Promote Reduce, Reuse, Recycle (3Rs)
Launch campaigns to minimize waste generation.
Incentivize use of reusable items (bags, bottles, containers).
Support businesses that use recycled materials.

✅ 3. Efficient Collection and Transportation
Schedule timely waste pickup services.
Use GPS-enabled trucks for efficient routing.
Introduce underground or smart bins with fill-level sensors.

✅ 4. Composting Organic Waste
Establish community and household composting units.
Convert kitchen and garden waste into fertilizer.
Reduce load on landfills by diverting biodegradable waste.

✅ 5. Waste-to-Energy (WTE) Technologies
Use incineration, anaerobic digestion, and gasification to convert waste into energy.
Properly monitor emissions to prevent air pollution.

✅ 6. Recycling Infrastructure
Build and support recycling centers.
Partner with informal waste pickers and formalize their role.
Offer incentives for recyclable waste collection.

✅ 7. Landfill Management
Modernize landfill sites to control leachate and methane emissions.
Practice sanitary landfilling rather than open dumping.
Cap and reclaim old landfills for other uses.

✅ 8. Public-Private Partnerships (PPP)
Involve private companies in waste management services.
Encourage investment in technology and infrastructure.

✅ 9. Strict Regulations and Penalties
Implement fines for littering and non-compliance.
Enforce rules for bulk waste generators (hotels, apartments, industries).

✅ 10. Citizen Awareness and Participation
Educate people through media, schools, and NGOs.
Conduct cleanliness drives and workshops.

## Data sources and AI methods
Smart bin sensors (fill-level data)
GPS data from garbage trucks
CCTV/Camera images from waste collection points
Public feedback via mobile apps
AI Techniques:
Image classification (for waste type detection using CNNs)
Route optimization algorithms (using reinforcement learning or graph search)
Prediction models (to forecast bin fill levels or waste volume trends)

## Challenges
Data Privacy: Surveillance for image analysis may raise concerns.
Hardware Costs: Smart bins and sensors can be expensive for low-income municipalities.
Segregation Accuracy: Misclassification can occur in poor lighting or mixed waste conditions.

## What next?
Expansion: Extend the solution to industrial waste or e-waste tracking.
Community Participation: Introduce gamified household waste segregation through mobile apps.
Integration: Link with carbon footprint calculators and environmental dashboards.
